Case study 1

Target Brazil — E-commerce Operations SQL Case Study

An end-to-end SQL analysis of 99,441 orders placed with Target in Brazil between September 2016 and October 2018. 30 BigQuery queries answer every question in the brief, then go further with six deep-dives into lateness and reviews, local vs cross-state fulfilment, seller coverage, categories, retention and peak-season delays. The work ends with eight ranked, evidence-backed recommendations, and one script rebuilds every result from the raw data.

Netflix — Content Strategy Analysis

What should Netflix produce next, and where should it grow? An end-to-end exploratory analysis of 8,807 movies and TV shows. I cleaned the catalogue, engineered features, un-nested actors, directors, countries and genres, then analysed trends, markets and launch timing across 23 charts. It shows a pivot to TV, a family-content gap, each country's content personality and fast-rising markets, and ends with ten recommendations.

Case study 3

Walmart — Black Friday Spending Analysis

Do women spend more than men on Black Friday? Confidence intervals and the Central Limit Theorem applied to 550,068 transactions. I ran bootstrap experiments at three sample sizes and built 90%, 95% and 99% intervals for gender, marital status and age. Men spend about 8% more per transaction, but product mix explains the whole gap: within the same category, women pay the same. It ends with ten recommendations for Walmart.

Case study 4

Aerofit — Treadmill Customer Profiling

Who buys which treadmill, and how should Aerofit recommend the right one? An exploratory analysis and probability study of 180 customers across three products. Using contingency tables, marginal and conditional probabilities, a Bayes check and chi-square tests, I profiled the buyer of each model. Income, usage and fitness decide who buys the premium KP781, while age and marital status don't change the choice.

Case study 5

Yulu — What Drives E-Cycle Demand

Yulu saw a dip in revenue, so this project uses 10,886 hourly rental records to find which factors really affect demand. I used t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square and regression, checked each parametric test's assumptions and confirmed results with non-parametric tests. Weather and season swing demand by 40-50%, hour of day is the dominant driver, and a regression on these drivers explains most of the variation.

Case study 6

Delhivery — Feature Engineering on Trip Data

Turns 144,867 raw logistics rows into a clean, analysis-ready table of 14,817 delivery trips. I rebuilt each trip with a two-stage merge, engineered features from facility names, time and routes, capped outliers, and encoded and scaled the data into 67 features. Paired hypothesis tests show real trips take about 2.1 times as long as the routing engine plans.

Case study 7

Jamboree — Graduate Admission Drivers

What decides an applicant's chance of getting into a top college abroad? A linear regression on 500 applicant profiles for Jamboree Education. I read coefficients and p-values with an OLS model and tested the assumptions of linear regression, including multicollinearity with VIF. CGPA, GRE and TOEFL matter most, while SOP and University Rating add little once the others are known.

Case study 8

LoanTap — Credit Risk Assessment

Should LoanTap extend a credit line to a given person? A logistic regression on 396,030 loan records. I explored the data, removed outliers, engineered and encoded features, then judged the model on precision and recall as well as accuracy. Interest rate, debt-to-income ratio and income are the strongest signals. Accuracy is 81%, but recall on defaulters is only 7%, and the page explains why and what to do next.
