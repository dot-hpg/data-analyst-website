Jamboree: predicting graduate admission

What decides an applicant's chance of getting into a top college abroad? A linear regression on 500 applicant profiles that measures which factors matter and predicts the chance of admission.

The brief

What the business case asked

Jamboree helps students prepare for exams like the GMAT, GRE and SAT and gain admission to top colleges abroad. It recently added a website feature that lets students estimate their chance of admission to Ivy League colleges, from the perspective of Indian applicants.

The case asks which factors are important in graduate admissions, how they relate to each other, and how well an applicant's chance of admission can be predicted from them.

Key findings

What the data showed

The evidence

Recommendations

What this means for Jamboree

1

Build the admission predictor on these features

With a test R² of 0.82 and an average error of about 4 percentage points, the model is a useful estimate for students.

2

Tell students to protect their CGPA

It is the strongest factor, and the model gets noticeably worse without it.

3

Keep focusing on GRE and TOEFL preparation

Both have a significant effect on the chance of admission, and this is Jamboree's core strength.

4

Encourage research and strong recommendations

Research experience and LOR strength both have a significant effect, though a smaller one.

5

Treat SOP and University Rating as secondary

Neither one adds much once the other factors are known, so they shouldn't drive the prediction.

Method

How I did the analysis

Exploration

Checked 500 rows and 9 columns, found no nulls or duplicates, dropped the serial number, and studied each feature against the target.

Preparation

An 80/20 train-test split, with features standardised using a scaler fitted on the training data only.

Modelling

An OLS model in statsmodels to read coefficients and p-values, alongside a scikit-learn LinearRegression for scoring.

Multicollinearity

Checked VIF for every feature, then tested the model without CGPA to confirm it is worth keeping.

Assumption checks

Residuals centred on zero and near normal, a linear relationship with the target, and no cone shape in errors against predictions.

Evaluation

MAE, RMSE, R² and adjusted R² on both training and test data. Test RMSE is 0.061.
