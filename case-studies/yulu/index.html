Yulu: what drives e-cycle demand?

Yulu, a shared e-cycle provider in India, saw a dip in revenue. Using 10,886 hourly rental records and hypothesis testing, I found which factors really affect demand and how well they explain it.

The brief

What the business case asked

Yulu's revenue had dipped, and the company wanted to understand what affects demand for its shared e-cycles. The case asks whether working days, weather and season change the number of rentals, and whether weather depends on season.

I checked every parametric test against its assumptions and confirmed it with a non-parametric alternative, then built a regression to measure how well these drivers explain demand overall.

Key findings

What the data showed

Hour of day

The dominant driver

Weekday demand peaks at 8 AM and 5-6 PM, following the commute.

−42%

Weather matters

Rentals fall in the order Clear, Mist, Light Rain, with light rain 42% below clear weather.

−50%

So does season

Rentals run Fall, then Summer and Winter, then Spring, which is 50% below Fall.

p = 0.226

Working days don't change volume

Total rentals stay the same, but working days reshape when people ride and who rides.

~81%

Registered users drive rides

Casual riders are about 2.4x more active at weekends than on weekdays.

+65%

Demand is growing

Demand rose 65% year on year, so the dip is seasonal or operational, not structural.

Recommendations

What the business should do

1

Rebalance the fleet before peaks

Move cycles to metro, bus and office zones before the 8 AM and 5-6 PM weekday peaks.

2

Win over weekend riders

Place cycles at leisure spots and run offers to turn casual riders into registered members.

3

Plan around weather and season

They swing demand by 40-50%. Use forecasts for daily rebalancing, and schedule maintenance in Spring and on rainy days.

4

Protect the revenue base

Registered users generate about 81% of rides, so offer commuter passes and loyalty rewards.

5

Size the fleet with a forecast

A forecasting model built on these drivers (R² 0.93) can size the fleet.

Method

How I did the analysis

Assumption checks

Shapiro-Wilk, Q-Q plots and Levene's test for every parametric test.

Non-parametric confirmation

Mann-Whitney U and Kruskal-Wallis back up the t-test and ANOVA results.

Effect sizes

Cohen's d, η² and Cramér's V show how big each effect is, not just whether it exists.

Post-hoc tests

Bonferroni-corrected comparisons show which groups really differ.

Regression

OLS on log(count) with hour × working day, season, weather, temperature and year.
