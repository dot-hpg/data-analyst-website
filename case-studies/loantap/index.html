LoanTap: who should get a credit line?

Given a set of attributes for a person, should LoanTap extend them a credit line? A logistic regression on 396,030 loan records for the underwriting of personal loans.

The brief

What the business case asked

LoanTap is an online platform offering customised loans to salaried professionals and businesses. Its data science team is building an underwriting layer to determine creditworthiness. This case focuses on the Personal Loan product.

The question is whether a credit line should be extended to a given person, and how to balance catching real defaulters against turning away good customers, since non-performing assets are a real problem in this industry.

Key findings

What the data showed

81 / 19

An imbalanced target

81% of customers fully paid their loan and 19% were charged off, so accuracy alone can mislead.

2.13

Interest rate is the top risk signal

The interest rate has the largest positive coefficient, meaning higher rates go with more defaults.

1.04

Debt load raises risk

A higher debt-to-income ratio is the second-strongest sign of default.

−1.14

Income lowers risk

Annual income has the strongest negative coefficient, so higher earners default less.

0.95

Loan amount and instalment overlap

These two features are 95% correlated. Public records and bankruptcies are 70% correlated.

7%

Few defaulters are caught

At the default threshold the model catches 771 of 11,399 defaulters, despite 81% accuracy.

The evidence

Recommendations

What this means for LoanTap

1

Don't disburse loans to everyone

About 1 in 5 borrowers in this data defaults, and those loans become non-performing assets.

2

Verify every applicant

Many customers in the data are not verified. Each applicant should be checked internally or by a third party.

3

Look closely at risky profiles

Take extra care with high interest rates and high debt-to-income ratios, and treat higher income as a positive sign.

4

Improve defaulter detection before relying on the model

At 7% recall it isn't ready to be used alone. Tune it, or try a stronger model.

Method

How I did the analysis

Exploration

Studied 27 columns with univariate and bivariate plots, and correlation checks against loan status.

Outliers

Found outliers in all numerical features with the IQR method, and removed about 5% of the data as potential outliers.

Feature engineering

Extracted the zip code from each address and dropped sub-grade, employment title and length, issue date, loan title, earliest credit line and address.

Encoding

Mapped term, loan status and list status to numbers, and one-hot encoded categories like grade, home ownership, purpose and zip code.

Modelling

An 80/20 split, MinMax scaling fitted on the training data, and a scikit-learn logistic regression.

Evaluation

Accuracy, precision, recall, F1, a confusion matrix, ROC AUC and the precision-recall curve.

An honest limitation

The report creates SMOTE-balanced data, but these results come from a model trained on the original imbalanced data, which is a big reason recall is so low. The clear next step is to train on the balanced data and tune the decision threshold.
