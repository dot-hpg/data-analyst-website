The brief

What the business case asked

Target is a major US retailer, and this case looks at its operations in Brazil. Acting as a data analyst at Target, the task was to analyse about 100,000 orders from 2016 to 2018, then extract insights and make actionable recommendations.

I answered every question in the brief with a query, then added six deep-dives of my own to explain why the numbers look the way they do.

Key findings

What the data showed

+137%

Growth came from volume

Order value rose from R$3.67M to R$8.69M (Jan-Aug 2017 vs 2018). Orders grew 135% while the average order stayed flat at about R$160.

Plateau

Growth has levelled off

Orders rose from 800 in Jan 2017 to a Black Friday peak of 7,544 in Nov 2017, then held at about 6.2-7.3k a month through 2018.

66.5%

Demand is concentrated

Two in three customers live in just three states (SP, RJ, MG), and 59.7% of sellers are in São Paulo.

3.5x

A wide delivery gap

Delivery takes 8.3 days in São Paulo and 29.0 days in Roraima, where freight is also 2.8x higher (R$42.98 vs R$15.15).

4.32 → 1.70

Lateness destroys reviews

Orders delivered 15+ days early average 4.32 stars. Orders 8+ days late average 1.70, and 70% of them get 1 star.

96.9%

Almost nobody returns

Nearly all customers ordered only once, so growth depends on winning new customers.

The SQL

Same state or cross state?

One of the deep-dives beyond the brief. It joins four tables to compare orders where the seller is in the customer's state with those shipped from elsewhere.

WITH item_level AS (
  SELECT
    o.order_id,
    CASE WHEN s.seller_state = c.customer_state
         THEN 'Same state' ELSE 'Cross state' END AS route,
    oi.freight_value,
    oi.price,
    TIMESTAMP_DIFF(o.order_delivered_customer_date,
                   o.order_purchase_timestamp, DAY) AS actual_days,
    CASE WHEN TIMESTAMP_DIFF(o.order_delivered_customer_date,
                             o.order_estimated_delivery_date, DAY) > 0
         THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS is_late
  FROM target.orders AS o
  JOIN target.customers   AS c  ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
  JOIN target.order_items AS oi ON o.order_id = oi.order_id
  JOIN target.sellers     AS s  ON oi.seller_id = s.seller_id
  WHERE o.order_status = 'delivered'
    AND o.order_delivered_customer_date IS NOT NULL
)
SELECT
  route,
  COUNT(*) AS items,
  ROUND(100 * COUNT(*) / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 2) AS pct_of_items,
  ROUND(AVG(actual_days), 2) AS avg_delivery_days,
  ROUND(AVG(freight_value), 2) AS avg_freight,
  ROUND(100 * SUM(freight_value) / SUM(price), 2) AS freight_pct_of_price,
  ROUND(100 * AVG(is_late), 2) AS pct_late
FROM item_level
GROUP BY route
ORDER BY items DESC;

Where the network struggles

Recommendations

Eight actions, ranked

1

Fix reliability before speed

A late order loses 0.9-2.6 review stars, while an early one gains almost nothing. Make on-time % the headline logistics KPI, and start with RJ (13.5% late, second-largest market).

2

Recalibrate delivery estimates per state

Estimates are padded by 11 days on average and up to 20 days in the North. Show realistic dates at checkout, and add buffer in AL and MA (20-24% late).

3

Localise supply

Same-state items arrive 2x faster with 43% lower freight. Recruit sellers in BA, PE and RJ, which have 70-180 customers per seller, and pilot a North-East cross-dock.

4

Make freight affordable in remote states

Freight is 22-29% of the item price in the North and North-East, against 14% in SP. Use bulk carrier contracts, subsidised shipping and free-shipping thresholds.

5

Plan peak capacity early

Black Friday 2017 brought 5x a normal day's orders, and late deliveries jumped to 12.4% that month. Pre-book carriers and staff from October, and widen estimates during peaks.

6

Grow basket size

Average order value has been flat at about R$160 for two years. Promote 10x interest-free plans, which already carry R$415 tickets, on high-ticket categories, and add bundles.

7

Build retention

97% of customers buy once. Loyalty programmes, post-delivery coupons and personalised recommendations are the cheapest growth available now that acquisition has plateaued.

8

Time marketing to the afternoon and evening

38% of orders arrive 13:00-18:00 and 28% at night. Focus flash sales and paid media on 13:00-22:00, and run maintenance at dawn.

Method

How I kept the work trustworthy

One source of truth

Each query lives once, in BigQuery SQL. A small translator rewrites the dialect-specific functions for DuckDB, and sqlglot parses every BigQuery query as a syntax check.

Fully reproducible

One command, python run_all.py, rebuilds the database, queries, charts, screenshots, report and notebook from the raw CSVs in about a minute.

Delivery maths from the brief

time_to_deliver is delivered minus purchased, and diff_estimated_delivery is delivered minus estimated, in whole days. An order is late when the difference is above zero.

Counting people, not orders

Customers are counted with customer_unique_id, because customer_id is created once per order and would hide repeat buyers.

Documented data cleaning

The reviews file's encoding, a renamed category column and duplicate geolocation rows are all handled and written up in the repo's data notes.
