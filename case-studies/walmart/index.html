Walmart Black Friday: do women spend more than men?

Confidence intervals and the Central Limit Theorem applied to 550,068 retail transactions, to generalise from a sample to 50 million male and 50 million female customers.

The brief

What the business case asked

Walmart's management wants to know whether spending habits differ between male and female customers on Black Friday, and how marital status and age affect spend.

I used bootstrapped sampling distributions and confidence intervals to move from a sample of transactions to conclusions about the wider customer population, then explained the result rather than stopping at the headline number.

Key findings

What the data showed

8% more

Men spend more per transaction

Men average 9,437.5 against 8,734.6 for women, a gap of about 703.

No overlap

The gap is statistically clear

Gender confidence intervals don't overlap at 90%, 95% or 99%. They overlap only with tiny samples (n = 300).

100%

Product mix explains the whole gap

Within the same category women pay the same as men. Re-weighting women's prices by men's basket mix closes the entire gap.

p = 0.73

Marital status makes no difference

9,261 vs 9,266, with overlapping intervals at every sample size and confidence level.

51+

Spend rises with age

From 8,934 for ages 0-17 up to 9,464 for ages 51+.

~990K

The most valuable customer is 26-35

Total Black Friday spend per customer is highest in this group. Across genders, men spend about 925K and women about 712K.

Recommendations

What the business should do

1

Win more women shoppers

Women are half the population but only about 28% of Black Friday customers.

2

Help women discover bigger-ticket products

Bundle Categories 5 and 8 with higher-value items.

3

Keep male shoppers loyal

They bring 77% of Black Friday revenue.

4

Don't price by marital status

Use it only to decide which products to show.

5

Focus the main campaign on ages 26-50

Treat 26-35 and 36-50 as one segment.

6

Premium offers for 51+

This group has the highest spend per item.

7

Reach teenagers through parents

Use family bundles.

8

Stock premium ranges in City C

It has the highest ticket size. Upsell in City A.

9

Never run out of Categories 1, 5 and 8

They make up about 74% of transactions.

10

Test on large samples

Use 95% confidence for big decisions.

Method

How I did the analysis

Data checks

Data types converted to category, no missing values, and 0.49% IQR outliers, all genuine premium purchases.

Gender analysis

Means, a Welch t-test, effect size and a product-mix decomposition of the gap.

CLT experiments

1,000 bootstrap means at n = 300, 3,000 and 30,000, with Q-Q normality checks and CI width against sample size.

Confidence intervals

90%, 95% and 99% intervals, using bootstrap percentile and analytical z, with automated overlap checks.

Other segments

The same analysis for married vs unmarried and for five life-stage age bins, plus ANOVA.

Reproducible

All sampling uses a fixed random seed, and the notebook reruns in about a minute.
