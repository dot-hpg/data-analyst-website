Aerofit: who buys which treadmill

Who buys which treadmill, and how should Aerofit recommend the right one? An exploratory analysis and probability study of 180 customers across three products.

The brief

What the business case asked

Aerofit sells three treadmills: the entry-level KP281 ($1,500), the mid-level KP481 ($1,750) and the advanced KP781 ($2,500). Its market research team wants to identify the target audience for each one, so new customers get better recommendations.

The analysis asks whether the products differ in customer characteristics, and what the marginal and conditional probabilities of buying each model are for different customer groups.

Key findings

What the data showed

100% vs 0%

Income is the strongest driver

Every buyer earning above $80K chose the KP781, and no buyer earning below $40K did.

80.8%

Frequent runners buy the KP781

That's the share among people who run 5-7 days a week. It is 65.5% among those with fitness of 4-5.

31.7% vs 9.2%

A gender gap at the premium end

Men are far more likely to buy the KP781, although female KP781 buyers are just as fit and affluent.

p > 0.05

Age and marital status don't matter

Chi-square tests show neither one changes which model people choose.

$250

The two cheaper models look alike

KP281 and KP481 buyers are nearly indistinguishable, so the price gap is the deciding factor.

30.8%

Small share, big revenue

The KP781 has only 22.2% of buyers but brings in 30.8% of revenue.

Recommendations

What the business should do

1

A three-question sales checklist

Days per week, fitness from 1 to 5, and budget map each customer to the right model.

2

Market the KP781 to women

Reach them through female athletes, running clubs and gyms.

3

Financing and trade-in offers

Bring fit, frequent runners earning $40K to $80K up to the KP781.

4

Reposition the KP481

Sell it as "the treadmill you won't outgrow" and bundle extras to justify the $250 step-up.

5

Keep the KP281 the easy starter

Aim it at students, first-job buyers and couples.

6

Household offers and upgrades

59% of buyers are partnered. Run upgrade campaigns for KP281 owners after 6 to 12 months.

Method

How I did the analysis

Basic metrics

Shape, data types, conversion to category and a statistical summary.

Non-graphical analysis

Value counts, unique values, product-level means and medians, and the revenue mix.

Missing values and outliers

Null and duplicate checks, IQR fences, mean vs median, boxplots and clipping sensitivity.

Visual analysis

Histograms with KDE, countplots, stacked bars, boxplots by product and gender, scatterplots, a correlation heatmap and a pairplot.

Probability

Contingency tables with marginal, joint and conditional probabilities for 7 attributes, plus a Bayes check.

Chi-square tests

Tests of independence show which customer attributes really change the choice of model.
